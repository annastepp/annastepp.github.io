Research focus

This statewide literacy program in the Southeastern U.S. mails free books to students in kindergarten through fifth grade. Most evaluations ask whether a program like this works on average. I want to know who it helps: which students, schools, and regions benefit, and which do not.

The data are longitudinal state assessments (FAST and Star) for about 1.4 million students over three school years, 2022–23 to 2024–25. Families enroll at different times, so the analysis has to account for students joining the program in different years.

What I have done

  • Data audit. Found that admission dates recorded batch data loads rather than real enrollment (32% of one year's roster shared a single date), which led the team to time treatment by school year instead.
  • Who enrolls. The score gap between students who later enroll and those who never do roughly quadruples at grade 3, consistent with families enrolling after high-stakes third-grade testing.
  • Trajectory clustering. Grouped 1.42 million students by the shape of their pre-enrollment score trajectories using k-means; enrollment rates differed threefold across groups.
  • Causal design. Designed a staggered difference-in-differences analysis (Callaway & Sant'Anna, 2021) and am estimating heterogeneous effects with causal forests by grade, English-learner status, and trajectory group.
